Where Whack-a-Mole comes from

Whack-a-Mole traces back to physical arcade cabinets from the 1970s, where players swung a mallet to hit mole toys popping randomly out of holes. The concept was later adapted into countless video games and mobile apps, and even became a common metaphor (“whack-a-mole management” describes problems that keep popping up — solve one and another appears). The online version swaps the mallet for a mouse click or a tap, but the core gameplay is exactly the same.

What this game tests

Whack-a-Mole looks simple, but it actually exercises several abilities at once: visual search (spotting a target quickly on screen), reaction time (how fast you act once you spot it), and hand-eye coordination (precisely moving your cursor or finger to the right spot to click). Compared to a pure reaction-time test, Whack-a-Mole adds the extra steps of spatial targeting and movement, which makes it closer to real-life situations that call for a fast response.

How to play

After tapping “Start Game,” moles pop up randomly from the 9 holes over 30 seconds — click one the moment you see it to score. Moles only stay up briefly, so react too slowly or click at the wrong moment and you'll miss. The game ends automatically when time runs out, showing your total score for that round, and it remembers your best score for this session.

Tips for a better score

Instead of staring at a single hole, keep your eyes near the center of the whole 3x3 grid and use your peripheral vision to catch movement all around it. Keep your cursor near the center of the screen to minimize travel distance; if you're on a touch device, using your index finger rather than your thumb usually gives you better accuracy.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you play Whack-a-Mole?
After pressing Start, moles pop up randomly from the 9 holes — click one as soon as you see it to score. Moles only stay up for a short moment before ducking back down, so react too slowly and you'll miss. The game runs for 30 seconds and ends automatically, showing your total score.
What skills does this game train?
Whack-a-Mole mainly trains visual search speed, reaction time, and hand-eye coordination — once your eyes spot the target, your hand (mouse or touch) has to move quickly and accurately click the right spot. Unlike a pure reaction-time test, it adds the extra steps of targeting and movement.
Why doesn't a click sometimes score even though I clearly hit a mole?
Moles only stay up for a very short time — if the mole has already ducked back down by the moment you click (even if the animation isn't fully finished on screen), it won't count. This is intentional, to prevent players from gaming the score by clicking wildly all over the screen.
Is there a way to make the moles appear faster and more often?
Yes — once your score crosses certain thresholds, the difficulty automatically levels up: moles stay up for less time, appear more frequently, and more can be active at once. Leveling up also adds bonus seconds to the clock, letting you keep pushing for a higher score.
What score counts as being good at this?
Most people can whack around 15-25 moles in 30 seconds. Scoring above 30 is quite fast reflexes, and over 35 means genuinely sharp speed and reaction time. Try a few rounds and aim to beat your own best score.
